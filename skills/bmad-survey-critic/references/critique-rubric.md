# Critique rubric

A critique earns blocking power only when it points to a plausible defect in a decision-relevant claim and names a verification action that could resolve or correctly preserve the uncertainty.

Use the debate contract's canonical critique types: `assumption`, `method`, `statistics`, `comparability`, `boundary`, `missing-alternative`, `contradiction`, and `source-support`. Represent causal-inference, selection-bias, and reproducibility concerns under the closest canonical type and name the specific concern in the challenge; do not create incompatible type values.

Use severity:

- `blocking`: could reverse a central conclusion, invalidate a safety claim, or change the decision recommendation.
- `major`: materially changes an important claim or section.
- `minor`: improves precision or metadata without changing the conclusion.
- `commentary`: rhetorical or stylistic observation with no verification request.

Test for scope drift, proxy metrics presented as outcomes, cherry-picked baselines, mismatched datasets or budgets, unreported uncertainty, claims stronger than study design, absence of negative results, hidden operating constraints, and correlated citations mistaken for replication.

Do not award severity for cleverness. A critique should be answerable by inspecting evidence, narrowing a claim, changing status, or explicitly recording stable disagreement. Its verification request names what result closes the critique, changes its severity, or stabilizes it as unresolved.
