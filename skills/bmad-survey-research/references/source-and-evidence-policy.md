# Source and evidence policy

Match evidence strength to claim strength. Prefer original papers, standards, official documentation, datasets, source code, and first-party benchmark artifacts for direct technical claims. Use systematic reviews and high-quality surveys for field maps; use vendor or practitioner material with explicit provenance and incentives.

An evidence card records its stable evidence ID, bibliographic identity or stable URL, source class, access status, exact section, page, table, figure, line, or commit, a brief paraphrase or minimum necessary excerpt, the supported claim IDs, applicability conditions, quality, and limitations. Respect access and quotation limits. An inaccessible or abstract-only item belongs under source leads, not evidence cards.

Claim statuses are `proposed`, `supported`, `disputed`, `incomparable`, `insufficient-evidence`, and `rejected`. A researcher assigns `proposed` to new claims and records a separate support assessment of `direct`, `partial`, `conflicting`, or `none`. Only verification and adjudication change a new claim to another status. Existing adjudicated statuses are preserved unless the research packet explicitly proposes a transition for later review.

For empirical claims capture dataset and version, split, metric definition and direction, model and checkpoint, training or tuning budget, hardware, precision, batch, sequence length, decoding or inference regime, and uncertainty. Missing context narrows what may be claimed.

Do not infer source content from a title, citation graph, abstract alone, or another author's summary. Record such items as leads. Separate evidence from interpretation and make every synthesis statement point back to claim IDs.
